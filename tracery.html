This Tracery project explores the foundational grammar of web development, serving as a generative primer for beginning coders. The code generates randomized scenarios that cover basic principles and common development roadblocks, such as syntax errors and broken pages. Currently, I’m at an expert level with broken pages. My exploration focuses on the role of markup languages. Drucker describes Markup languages as “sets of codes, embodied in tags, that are used to analyze the contents of textual documents” (2021, p. 61). Common markup languages include XML, TEI, KML, and JSON. For a beginner, however, the most immediate hurdle is the web’s basic structure. Before trying to figure out why an image won't load or hunting down a single missing closing tag, one must understand the building blocks. Focusing on that foundation, Drucker notes that "HTML is a descriptive language that identifies the structural elements of a document. All HTML documents are structured with tags-standard terms inserted between angle brackets that are actually instructions” (2021, p. 35). By recombining these structural instructions with the errors that result from misusing them, the project highlights how digital environments require both rigid logic and creative problem-solving.

Drucker, J. (2021). The digital humanities coursebook: An introduction to digital methods for research and scholarship. Routledge.
